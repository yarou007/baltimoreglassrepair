Common glass emergencies we handle in Baltimore

Emergency glass repair covers a wide range of situations. Here are the most common calls we receive from Baltimore homes and businesses:

  • Vandalism and break-ins: Shattered storefronts, smashed door glass, and broken entry panels after forced entry or deliberate damage. These require immediate securing to prevent further loss and liability.
  • Storm damage: High winds, falling trees, and flying debris can crack or shatter windows and storefronts. Baltimore's weather can shift quickly—especially during spring and fall storm seasons.
  • Vehicle impact: Cars backing into storefront glass, delivery trucks hitting door frames, and parking lot accidents that damage ground-floor commercial glass.
  • Accidental breakage: Dropped equipment, construction impact, and accidents inside commercial spaces or homes that crack or shatter glass panes.
  • Unsafe glass conditions: Cracked tempered glass panels that could collapse unexpectedly, loose shards in door frames, or broken glass creating a hazard for staff, customers, or residents.

Our emergency response process

When you call 703-244-0559, here's what happens:

  1. Phone triage: We ask for your address, the best entry point, whether the building is occupied or alarmed, and a brief description of the damage. Photos help but are not required.
  2. Dispatch: We send a crew with the right tools and common glass stock. ETA is typically 20–60 minutes depending on your location, traffic, and weather conditions.
  3. Site assessment: On arrival, we evaluate the damage, identify safety hazards, and determine whether same-day repair is possible or if emergency board up is the right first step.
  4. Secure the opening: If replacement glass is not available immediately, we board up the opening to protect against weather, debris, and unauthorized entry.
  5. Repair or schedule replacement: For standard sizes, we can often replace the glass the same day. For custom sizes, we measure accurately, order the correct glass, and schedule installation.
  6. Documentation: We provide clear descriptions of damage and work performed for your records and insurance documentation.

Before we arrive — what you should do

While waiting for our crew, there are a few things you can do to stay safe:

  • Keep people, pets, and children away from broken glass and the damaged area
  • Do not attempt to tape large shattered panels — especially tempered glass that has fractured into small cubes
  • If the opening is ground-level and weather or security is a concern, you can hang a tarp or heavy plastic sheeting from the inside as a temporary measure
  • If a crime occurred (break-in, vandalism), file a police report before cleanup — your insurance carrier will likely require it
  • Take photos of the damage from a safe distance if you can — this helps with insurance claims and our assessment

Commercial vs residential emergencies

Commercial emergencies often involve tenant coordination, public access concerns, and business continuity pressure. A broken storefront on a busy Baltimore street needs to be secured before the next business day. A shattered office entry door needs to be safe for employees arriving in the morning.

Residential emergencies may prioritize different concerns: heat loss in winter, security in ground-floor units, and keeping children and pets safe. We use the same 24/7 phone line for both — with different checklists and approaches based on the situation.

Emergency Glass Repair FAQ

How quickly can you arrive for emergency glass repair in Baltimore?

Arrival time depends on traffic, weather, and crew availability. In many emergency calls across Baltimore City and Baltimore County, we can arrive within 20–60 minutes. Call 703-244-0559 for a real-time ETA.

What should I do while waiting for emergency glass repair?

Keep people and pets away from broken glass. Do not attempt to tape large shattered panels—especially tempered glass that has fractured into small pieces. If the opening is ground-level and weather or security is a concern, you can hang a tarp from the inside while waiting for our crew.

Do you board up before replacing the glass?

When replacement glass is not available immediately—due to custom sizing, after-hours supplier closures, or fabrication lead time—we secure the opening with a professional board up first. We then return to install the correct replacement glass once it is ready.

Can you help with insurance documentation for emergency glass damage?

Yes. We provide clear descriptions of the damage, photos when appropriate, and detailed work summaries that you can submit to your insurance carrier for claims processing.

Do you handle both commercial and residential emergencies?

Yes. We respond to both commercial emergencies—such as broken storefronts, office entry doors, and restaurant glass—and residential emergencies including broken home windows, door glass, and patio doors. The same 24/7 phone line covers both.
