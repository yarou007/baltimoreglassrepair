White Marsh Commercial Glass: A Tenant Approval Checklist

Coordinate the tenant, owner, site contact and replacement scope before arranging commercial glass work in White Marsh.

By Baltimore Glass Repair · November 13, 2026

A site contact is not always the approving contact

For a White Marsh business unit, the person who opens the premises may not be the person authorized to approve storefront work. Separate site access from ownership and payment responsibilities early. This is especially useful when the damaged glass is part of an exterior storefront or a shared building component.

Review the lease or ask the property manager who controls the affected opening. Do not assume responsibility based only on who discovered the damage. If the opening is exposed, explain the urgency while confirming who can authorize temporary protection.

Name the opening precisely

Record the address and unit number, the location within the building and the type of panel. A fixed display pane, door lite and transom can require different handling and materials. If several panels are affected, identify them separately rather than combining them into one approximate measurement.

Keep staff and customers away from damaged glass. Note whether a door will not close, a frame has shifted or an access route is affected. These details help avoid treating a wider repair as a simple panel swap.

Agree on the commercial scope

  • Who authorizes the initial assessment and any temporary board-up?
  • Who approves the permanent replacement quote?
  • Are removal, disposal, glazing materials and installation included?
  • Does frame or door-hardware work require separate approval?
  • Who receives updates if measurements or site conditions change the scope?

Arrange building access and work timing

Share operating hours, keyholder details, delivery restrictions and any requirements for access to shared spaces. A replacement panel may need a safe handling route that differs from the route used for ordinary deliveries. Ask what the site needs to provide before confirming a visit.

If a building requires contractor documents or a work permit, raise that requirement with the service provider before scheduling. Do not assume that a website statement satisfies a specific building’s access rules.

Document the handover without assuming coverage

Keep the agreed quote, approval contact and description of the affected opening together. Where an insurance claim is involved, ask the insurer about documentation and coverage; neither a repair quote nor a board-up visit establishes that a claim will be paid. Confirm how temporary protection and final installation will be recorded.
